# Initial Customer Acquisition Channels

Goal for launch: do **not** try to become famous. Try to find the first **3 paying recurring clients**.

## Highest-priority tests

### 1. Existing personal/professional network — free
Send the referral message in `outreach_copy.md` to selected former colleagues, teachers, parents/acquaintances, and community contacts who may know students.

Why first: trust transfers much faster through a referral than through a brand-new website.

### 2. Luach.com classifieds — usually free in many categories
Luach.com specifically serves Orthodox Jewish communities including the Five Towns and NYC and says most classified categories are free to post.

Action:
- Post the short tutoring ad.
- Link to the site once live.
- Track inquiries as source = Luach.

### 3. The Jewish Home (Five Towns community publication)
The Jewish Home is a weekly Five Towns community publication distributed across Five Towns, Far Rockaway, Queens, Brooklyn, and other nearby communities.

Current ad/classified contacts found during launch research:
- Advertising: ads@fivetownsjewishhome.com
- Classifieds: classifieds@fivetownsjewishhome.com
- Classified text line: 443-929-4003
- General phone: 516-734-0858

Action:
- Ask for the smallest/lowest-cost tutoring classified option first.
- Do not buy a large display ad before testing response.

### 4. Five Towns / Queens community groups — free or low cost
Use relevant Facebook, WhatsApp, synagogue/community, neighborhood, school-parent, and local bulletin groups where commercial posts are allowed.

Action:
- Use the community post in `outreach_copy.md`.
- Do not spam many groups at once.
- Track each group separately so we learn which channel works.

### 5. The Advertiser (Far Rockaway / Five Towns)
A local direct-mail advertising publication serving Far Rockaway, Five Towns, and surrounding Jewish communities.

Action:
- Request the current smallest ad/classified rate.
- Test only after free/referral channels, unless the price is very low.

## Secondary acquisition tests

### Marketplace profile
Use a tutoring marketplace only as a lead source, not as the whole business. Price high enough that the marketplace fee still leaves an acceptable rate.

### Short educational content
Create a few very short useful posts such as:
- “Three SAT Math mistakes strong students still make”
- “What a p-value actually means”
- “How to know whether your child understands Algebra II or is memorizing procedures”

Each post ends with a simple tutoring CTA and site link.

### School/teacher referrals
Ask selected educators whether they keep a private tutor referral list. This is higher-trust than cold ads.

## What not to spend money on yet
- Large newspaper display ads
- Broad Google Ads campaigns
- Paid social campaigns with no tested offer
- SEO agency
- Logo/branding agency
- Custom tutoring marketplace software

First prove that at least a few people will pay the desired rate.

## Launch metrics
Track weekly:
- outreach messages sent
- posts/classifieds placed
- site visits if available
- inquiries
- qualified inquiries
- sessions booked
- sessions paid
- recurring clients
- effective acquisition cost
- reason prospects declined (price / schedule / subject / no response / other)
